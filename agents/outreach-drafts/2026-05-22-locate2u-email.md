Steve,

You have already shipped AI Agents for dispatch, customer service, and quoting, with a 35 percent dispatch-time claim. As an ASX-listed group, those numbers also have to hold up under scrutiny, which raises the bar in a useful way.

Two things tend to matter most after a fast AI launch. First, instrumentation. Knowing precisely which agent actions drive the dispatch and resolution gains, so the metrics are defensible and the roadmap is evidence-led, not vibe-led. Second, agent-readiness in the other direction. As your customers' own systems get more agentic, whether Locate2u can be orchestrated by external agents, not just run its own internally, decides who becomes part of the workflow and who gets routed around.

For a public company, the difference between 'we shipped AI' and 'we can prove and extend it' is material.

I run a practice that maps both for B2B SaaS at $500K to $5M ARR.

Worth 20 minutes to compare notes on Locate2u specifically?

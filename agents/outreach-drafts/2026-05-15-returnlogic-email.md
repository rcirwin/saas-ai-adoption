Subject: Return-reason analytics is the moat that nobody else has built

Peter,

The ReturnLogic arc ($2.3M to $2.8M ARR with the Series A from Mercury) and the D2C brand customer list (Beachwaver, Outdoor Research, The Sak) is real footprint in a workflow-heavy category. Return-reason analytics is the genuinely differentiated piece of the product.

The reason I am writing is the AI conversation in the category. Loop and Happy Returns are racing on exchange UX and credit incentives. AfterShip and ReturnGO are adding ML for return-reason classification. None of those moves get at the actual depth problem in returns: the data exists, but turning it into policy decisions and merchandising signals is still a human workflow.

The audit I run with founders in this position is the depth question. AI inside ReturnLogic that takes return-reason data and proposes SKU-level changes, returns-policy adjustments, supplier-quality signals. That is what return-reason analytics is for, and it is exactly the workflow AI nobody else in the category has built. Classifier features are catching up on the surface, not the depth.

Worth 20 minutes to compare notes on what that looks like for ReturnLogic specifically?

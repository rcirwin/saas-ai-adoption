Subject: Manual forecasting, and inFlow's reorder decisions

Louis,

Running inFlow bootstrapped from Toronto since 2007 to 40,000-plus businesses across 90 countries is a rare durability story in inventory software.

Two things stand out from a workflow lens. Demand forecasting is still manual and reorder decisions are rules-based, which means the customer carries the judgment AI could carry. And the split across Inventory, Stockroom and Manufacturing means a user's job often spans products, so the friction lives in the seams.

I run a practice that helps B2B SaaS teams figure out which AI opportunities are worth building and which to skip. It starts with a workflow and pain-point audit, not a model. For a product with this much surface area, knowing where AI does not belong is as useful as knowing where it does.

Worth a 20-minute call to see if it is relevant for inFlow?

Jonathon,

The Billables integration was a sharp move. It got AI-drafted time entries into LeanLaw without you building the model. For the time-capture slice, that is the right call.

The strategic risk is that partner-supplied AI is hard to differentiate on. Clio is going aggressively AI-native, and "we integrate with an AI tool" is a weaker position than "AI is built into how our platform works." The question is which AI genuinely belongs to LeanLaw: catching revenue leakage before it happens, checking trust-accounting moves against the rules in 50 states, drafting invoice narratives that read like a partner wrote them. Those sit on workflows you own and Billables does not.

I run a practice that helps B2B SaaS teams figure out which AI to build natively, which to partner for, and which to skip, based on real user workflow research.

Darby,

Most generative legal tools are racing to draft documents. For Clerky that is the wrong race. Your customers stayed because the docs are right, and an LLM that is confidently wrong on a SAFE is a liability, not a feature.

The questions worth asking are quieter. Where can AI accelerate the parts of the workflow that are not the legal judgment itself: gathering inputs, flagging inconsistencies, prepping the attorney review rather than replacing it. And separately, when a founder asks ChatGPT to "help me incorporate," can it reach Clerky cleanly, or does it route them somewhere less accurate?

I run a practice that helps B2B SaaS teams figure out which AI opportunities fit the workflow and which to skip, grounded in user research. As an attorney you would set the accuracy bar; I would help find where AI can sit under it.

Subject: When agents replace sequences

Brice,

The LGM arc (self-financed, 20 to 27 employees this year, 4.9 stars at 4.6M ARR) is genuinely rare. Multichannel breadth across LinkedIn, email, X, and calls with waterfall enrichment is a real moat against single-channel AI cold tools.

The reason I am writing is what Clay is doing to the category. Their AI agents are not better sequences. They are a different primitive. Instead of "set up a sequence, let AI personalize," the agent decides recipient, channel, timing, and message in one pass. Smartlead and Apollo are running the same play with less polish. That reframes "multichannel engagement" from a feature set to a constraint on the agent.

The audit I have been running with founders in this position is not "ship more AI features." It is the orchestration question. What does LGM look like rebuilt agent-first vs. sequence-first. Where does the multichannel breadth become an advantage for the agent (more decision surface) vs. a complexity tax. That answer is product-strategy work, not feature roadmap work.

Worth 20 minutes to compare notes on what that looks like for LGM specifically?

Every competitor in your space shipped an AI feature last quarter. You didn't.

That feels like falling behind. Usually it isn't.

Look at what most of those features actually are. A chat box bolted onto an existing screen. A "summarize" button. A suggestion the user still has to go verify themselves.

Shipping fast looks like progress. On a roadmap it reads as motion. In the market it's noise until it changes what the product actually does for the user.

The panic move is to copy the feature so you're not the only one without it.
The clarity move is to ask what job your users are trying to finish, and whether any of those features finish it.

Because the buyer's question is quietly shifting. It's moving from "can this help my team do X?" to **"does this just do X?"**

A me-too AI feature loses on both counts. It doesn't help much, and it doesn't do the job.

I'd take one feature that completes a workflow end to end in six months over five features that suggest and summarize by Friday.

And shipping the wrong thing isn't a neutral bet. It burns the engineering time, the roadmap slot, and the credibility you spend explaining it to the board next quarter.

**Clarity is the rarest asset in the room right now.** Not speed.

You're not behind for skipping the feature everyone copied. You're behind if you still can't name the job your product should own.

What's the one workflow your users would pay you to finish for them, not just help them do?

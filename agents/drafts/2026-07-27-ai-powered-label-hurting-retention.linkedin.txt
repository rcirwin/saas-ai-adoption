The "AI-powered" badge on your site is doing more damage to your retention than the feature behind it.

Not because the AI is bad. Because of the promise the label makes on your behalf.

A buyer who reads "AI-powered" doesn't hear "we added a model." They hear "this does the work." That becomes the bar renewal gets measured against a year later, and almost nobody sets that bar on purpose.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗺𝗶𝘀𝗲. Your team shipped a summarizer, a suggestion panel, a smart field somewhere in settings. The buyer read the badge and expected the job to come back finished. At renewal they aren't comparing your product to last year's version. They're comparing it to what the badge implied.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗮𝗿𝗶𝘀𝗼𝗻 𝘀𝗲𝘁. The label also moves you out of your own category. Once a product says "AI-powered," it stops getting benchmarked against the other three vendors on the shortlist and starts getting benchmarked against the contractor or the agency the buyer would have hired instead. Per-seat pricing looks strange inside that comparison. You're charging for access while implying you deliver a result.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝗶𝗴𝗻𝗮𝗹. Powered. Enabled. Assisted. Augmented. Every one of those words is a modifier, and every modifier quietly confirms that a human still does the work here. Buyers have started reading them that way. The badge that was supposed to say "we're ahead" increasingly reads as "we're a tool with a model bolted on."

None of this is an argument for hiding the AI. It's an argument that the label is downstream of the decision, not a substitute for making it.

Your team isn't scared AI is going to replace them.

They're scared the thing they're best at stops counting.

Those are different fears.

They need different answers.

Most founders answer the first one.

"Your job is safe."

It's meant kindly and it lands badly. Everyone in the room knows you can't promise that, and now they've learned you say comfortable things when you're unsure.

Here's a version of the second fear, anonymized.

A support lead who spent four years learning which refund requests are real. There's no SOP for it. It lives in her head.

When the AI pilot got announced, what she heard was: the part of my job that's hard to copy is the part nobody ever wrote down.

She was right about the first half. Wrong about the second.

What's in her head is exactly what the model doesn't have.

Model capability is getting cheap. Domain context isn't.

That's the bottleneck on almost every AI project I've looked at.

So the sentence isn't "you're safe."
It's "𝘆𝗼𝘂'𝗿𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝗽𝘂𝘁."

Then make it concrete the same week.

Pick one workflow. Ask the person who's best at it to write down what they check before they sign off.

Every exception. Every "it depends." Every time they overrode the obvious answer.

That document is the start of a harness.

And it's the most credible way to tell someone their judgment is the asset. You're not auditing them. You're asking them to design the thing that scales them.

To be clear, this isn't a promise that nothing changes. Some roles compress. Pretending otherwise is the same comfortable answer wearing a different shirt.

But the people who write the rules of the system are rarely the ones it routes around.

Your board asked about AI on the last call. You said you're evaluating. They wrote it down.

That answer works once. It doesn't work twice.

Here's what I'd say on the next call instead. Not a stall. A position.

**Name the bet you ruled out.** Boards don't lose confidence because you haven't shipped. They lose it when you can't say what you decided against. "We're not shipping a copilot this year, and here's the reasoning" lands better than "we're exploring a few directions." Ruling something out is proof the work happened.

**Bring the workflow, not the feature.** Your customer's job has seven steps. Your product probably owns one or two of them. That's the number your board should hear, because it's the number that decides whether you get routed around. "We own 2 of 7 today, and the plan gets us to 6" is a strategy. "We're adding AI summaries" is a roadmap slot.

**Commit to a decision date, not a ship date.** Something like "by October we'll know whether this workflow supports outcome-based pricing." Real deadline, falsifiable, and it gives the board something to hold you to that isn't a demo.

Most founders hear the AI question as "what are you shipping?" It's usually closer to "do you understand where the value in your category is moving?"

One of those you answer with a demo.
The other you answer with a thesis.

**Your board isn't underwriting a feature. They're underwriting your judgment about where the category is going.**

This doesn't buy unlimited time, to be clear. Two quarters of "we're still deciding" without a decision is its own answer.

What's the AI bet you've already ruled out, and does your board know why you ruled it out?

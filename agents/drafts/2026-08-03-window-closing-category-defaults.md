Your category's defaults are being written right now. Not by you. By whatever agents can reach today.

Most founders are still asking whether agents will show up in their market. That's the wrong question. They're already there, and they're learning how the job gets done.

Three things get decided in that window.

**The default path.** When an agent picks up a job in your category, it takes the cheapest reliable route to a finished result. Whatever works the first few times becomes the route it repeats. That pattern is forming now, in the open, without your input.

**The vocabulary.** An agent reasons about your category in the language it can find. Docs, APIs, schemas, public write-ups. If a competitor named the steps of the workflow and you didn't, the agent inherits their model of the work, including where your product is supposed to sit in it.

**The escalation rule.** For every step, the agent decides between calling a tool and doing it itself. Once "do it myself" becomes the default for a step, you're not competing to be chosen anymore. You're competing to be reconsidered. That's a much harder sale.

This is the timing argument at the category level. The first arbitrage, pointing a raw model at a task and trusting it, is closing as models commoditize. The second one, encoding real domain judgment into a harness, is open right now and compounding.

The window isn't "will agents matter." It's the gap between agents being able to work in your category and agents settling on how. That gap is where defaults get set, and it doesn't stay open long.

I could be early on the exact timing. I don't think I'm wrong about the direction.

**Whoever completes the job first sets the pattern everyone else gets measured against.**

Which step in your workflow would an agent already choose to do itself rather than call you for?

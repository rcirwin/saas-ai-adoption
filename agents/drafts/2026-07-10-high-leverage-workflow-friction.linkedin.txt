Not all workflow friction is worth solving with AI.

Most of it is just bad UX in a costume. A slow screen, one click too many, a label nobody understands. That's a design fix, not an AI opportunity.

The friction worth building around is rarer. It's the moment the workflow leaves your product and a human quietly applies judgment you never captured. That's where a harness compounds. Everything else is polish.

Here's the test I run to find it.

𝗗𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗶𝘁 𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗲𝗮𝘁? Encode judgment where the loop runs weekly, not once a quarter. Rare pain isn't worth a harness. Frequent pain pays back every cycle.

𝗗𝗼𝗲𝘀 𝗶𝘁 𝗲𝗻𝗱 𝗶𝗻 𝗮𝗻 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗼𝗿𝘁? Where users dump your data into a spreadsheet is where your product stops and their real work starts. Follow the export. Whatever they do in that sheet is the job you're not completing.

𝗜𝘀 𝗶𝘁 𝗮 𝗱𝗲𝗰𝗶𝘀𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗼𝗿 𝗷𝘂𝘀𝘁 𝗰𝗹𝗶𝗰𝗸𝘀? Too many steps is a UX problem. A choice they dread making is a harness problem.
One you fix with design. The other you fix by encoding how your best users decide.

𝗗𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘆 𝗼𝘄𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗼𝘂𝘁𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗲 𝗮𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗿? If finishing this step still hands them a half-done job, you automated 1 of 7. Find the friction that lets you complete all 7 and hand back the result.

Run those four before you build anything.
Three yeses means you found real leverage. One yes means you found a design ticket.

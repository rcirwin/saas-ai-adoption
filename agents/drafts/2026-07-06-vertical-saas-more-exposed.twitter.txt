1/ For a decade, vertical SaaS was the safe bet. Deep niche, sticky workflow, hard to rip out.

In the agent era, that's exactly what makes it exposed.

---

2/ The logic used to run the other way.

A tool built for one industry fit the job so tightly a generic competitor couldn't touch it.

Depth was the moat.

---

3/ But depth toward a single, well-defined job is also what makes you absorbable.

---

4/ Most vertical tools own one slice of a domain workflow.

The estimate. The intake form. The compliance check.

One step out of seven. Fine when a human ran the other six.

---

5/ An agent doesn't stop at one step. It's trying to complete the whole job.

A single, well-scoped slice is the easiest thing for it to absorb. Especially once it has the domain context you assumed only you had.

---

6/ Horizontal tools are broad substrate. The agent calls them across a hundred workflows.

Vertical tools sit inside the one workflow it's trying to finish. That puts them in the blast radius.

---

7/ The part most vertical founders miss:

Your domain depth isn't worthless. It's the raw material for the only durable position left.

But it only counts if you complete the job, not narrate one step of it.

---

8/ Look at EvenUp in personal injury law.

It didn't ship a better slice of the demand-letter process. It completed the letter.

That's the move.

---

9/ Owning one slice of a niche workflow used to be a moat.

Now it's a target.

The vertical players who survive won't own a step. 𝗧𝗵𝗲𝘆'𝗹𝗹 𝗰𝗼𝗺𝗽𝗹𝗲𝘁𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗷𝗼𝗯.

---

10/ If an agent could finish your users' entire job tomorrow, are you the step it absorbs, or the system it runs on?

A chatbot is what most teams build when they don't want to solve the actual problem.

It feels like progress. A box, a cursor, a fast answer. But watch what it does the moment a real user shows up.

It suggests. It hands back a paragraph and sends the user right back into the same workflow to go do the thing themselves.

The question a user actually has is almost never "tell me what to do." It's "do it."

When someone opens your product, they have a job to finish. A report to reconcile. A campaign to fix. A ticket queue to clear. A chatbot answers a question about that job. 𝗜𝘁 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗺𝗼𝘃𝗲 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗷𝗼𝗯 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝗰𝗵 𝗰𝗹𝗼𝘀𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗼 𝗱𝗼𝗻𝗲.

That's the gap buyers have started to feel. The question they ask is shifting from "can this help my team do X?" to "does this just do X?" A chat box lands on the wrong side of that line almost every time.

Three tells that a chatbot is standing in for real product work:

- It outputs advice, not a finished step.
- It makes the user translate the answer back into action.
- You could delete it tomorrow and the core workflow wouldn't change.

None of that makes chat useless. For open-ended exploration, it's a fine interface. But most product questions aren't open-ended. They're a specific job that needs finishing, and a suggestion isn't a finish.

The better move is quieter and harder. Pick the workflow your users hate most. Take the input, do the work, hand back the result. No paragraph to interpret. No next step to figure out.

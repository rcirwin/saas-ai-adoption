1/ Your category's defaults are being written right now.

Not by you. By whatever agents can reach today.

---

2/ Most founders are still asking whether agents will show up in their market.

Wrong question. They're already there, and they're learning how the job gets done.

---

3/ The default path.

When an agent picks up a job in your category, it takes the cheapest reliable route to a finished result.

Whatever works the first few times becomes the route it repeats. That pattern is forming now, without your input.

---

4/ The vocabulary.

An agent reasons about your category in the language it can find. Docs, APIs, schemas, public write-ups.

If a competitor named the steps of the workflow and you didn't, the agent inherits their model of the work.

---

5/ The escalation rule.

For every step, the agent decides between calling a tool and doing it itself.

Once "do it myself" becomes the default for a step, winning it back is a much harder sale.

---

6/ You're not competing to be chosen anymore.

You're competing to be reconsidered.

---

7/ This is the timing argument at the category level.

Arbitrage 1, pointing a raw model at a task and trusting it, is closing as models commoditize.

---

8/ Arbitrage 2, encoding real domain judgment into a harness, is open right now.

It compounds. Every model improvement makes the harness better.

---

9/ The window isn't "will agents matter."

It's the gap between agents being able to work in your category and agents settling on how.

That gap is where defaults get set. It doesn't stay open long.

---

10/ I could be early on the exact timing. I don't think I'm wrong about the direction.

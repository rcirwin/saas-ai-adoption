You don't need an AI hire yet.

You need a plan.

I keep watching SaaS teams post the same job: "Senior AI Engineer." Six figures, three months to fill, another month to onboard. Then they hand that person a blank page and a board that wants AI "by Q3."

A hire solves *how* to build. It does nothing for *what* to build. And the expensive mistakes almost always live in the *what*.

Here's the pattern I keep seeing:

- The roadmap is a wishlist of features that demo well, not workflows that hurt.
- The data the model needs is fragmented across three systems nobody mapped.
- The judgment that would make the AI useful is locked in two people's heads, undocumented.

None of that gets fixed by a stronger engineer. The bottleneck isn't model intelligence or talent. It's the absence of a plan that says which workflow to own, what context the model actually needs, and where the human judgment lives.

Hire first, and you pay someone six figures to discover all of this on the job. Plan first, and the hire walks in on day one with a blueprint instead of a blank page.

So I'm not saying never hire. I'm saying sequence it. The best AI hires I've seen were brought in to execute a strategy that already existed, not to invent one under board pressure.

If your AI roadmap is still a list of features instead of a plan for owning a workflow, what would your new hire actually build in week one?

Not all workflow friction is worth solving with AI.

Most of it is just bad UX in a costume. A slow screen, one click too many, a label nobody understands. That's a design fix, not an AI opportunity.

The friction worth building around is rarer. It's the moment the workflow leaves your product and a human quietly applies judgment you never captured. That's where a harness compounds. Everything else is polish.

Here's the test I run to find it.

**Does it repeat?** Encode judgment where the loop runs weekly, not once a quarter. Rare pain isn't worth a harness. Frequent pain pays back every cycle.

**Does it end in an export?** Where users dump your data into a spreadsheet is where your product stops and their real work starts. Follow the export. Whatever they do in that sheet is the job you're not completing.

**Is it a decision or just clicks?** Too many steps is a UX problem. A choice they dread making is a harness problem.
One you fix with design. The other you fix by encoding how your best users decide.

**Do they own the outcome after?** If finishing this step still hands them a half-done job, you automated 1 of 7. Find the friction that lets you complete all 7 and hand back the result.

Run those four before you build anything.
Three yeses means you found real leverage. One yes means you found a design ticket.

Workflow first. AI second.

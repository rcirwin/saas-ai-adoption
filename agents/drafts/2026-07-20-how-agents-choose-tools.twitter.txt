1/ The most important buyer in your funnel is about to stop being a person.

An agent will decide which SaaS tools to invoke to get the user's job done. And it grades you on criteria your roadmap ignores.

---

2/ For 20 years, winning a category meant winning a human.

Better UI. Faster onboarding. Cleaner dashboards. You competed for attention and clicks.

---

3/ That buyer is being replaced.

The user hands the job to an agent. The agent decides which tools it actually calls.

---

4/ Here's the part that should worry you:

The agent doesn't care about anything you optimized for.

---

5/ It doesn't see your onboarding. It doesn't read your marketing page. It feels no loyalty to your brand.

It asks three questions.

---

6/ Can I call this directly, or do I have to drive a human interface to use it?

---

7/ Does the output finish the job, or hand me back a half-step I still have to complete myself?

---

8/ Is invoking this tool cheaper and more reliable than just doing the work myself?

---

9/ Own 1 of 7 steps and need a human to drive it? The agent routes around you.

It stitches together the pieces it can call and rebuilds your thin slice in-house.

---

10/ This is the competition claim, one layer deeper.

You're not competing with the other SaaS in your category. You're competing with the agent's option to skip you entirely.

---

11/ The survivors won't have the best interface.

They'll be the ones an agent can call, that return a finished result, priced on the outcome instead of the seat.

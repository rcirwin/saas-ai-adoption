The worst question you can ask in a user interview is "what's frustrating about this?"

Users answer it honestly. That's the problem.

You get a tidy answer about a slow load or a missing filter. Real friction isn't there. It's the stuff they've routed around so many times they've stopped registering it as friction at all.

I've run these interviews across three data-heavy SaaS products, mostly Amazon seller tools. Long workflows, real money riding on every step. Three questions do most of the work:

**1. Ask for the last time, not the usual time.**
"Walk me through the last time you did this." The "usually" version is a cleaned-up story they've told before. The specific version has the alt-tabs, the second monitor, and the part where they messaged a coworker to check something.

**2. Follow the workaround, not the complaint.**
"What did you do right before that, and right after?" A complaint tells you what annoys someone. A workaround tells you what cost them enough that they built a system to avoid it. The workaround is a receipt.

**3. Ask what they check before they trust it.**
"Before you act on this number, what do you look at?" Most interviews skip this one. The answer is the judgment step, and it's almost never written down anywhere.

The first two questions find the friction. The third finds the expertise.

That third answer is where the AI work actually starts. The bottleneck was never model intelligence. It's that the domain context you need is sitting in your users' heads, undocumented, and nobody has asked for it directly.

**You can't encode judgment you never collected.**

What's the last thing a user told you they double-check before they trust a number in your product?

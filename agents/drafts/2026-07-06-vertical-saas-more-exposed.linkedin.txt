For a decade, vertical SaaS was the safe bet. Deep niche, sticky workflow, hard to rip out.

In the agent era, that's exactly what makes it exposed.

The logic used to run the other way. A tool built for one industry fit the job so tightly that a generic competitor couldn't touch it. Depth was the moat.

But depth toward a single, well-defined job is also what makes you 𝗮𝗯𝘀𝗼𝗿𝗯𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲.

Most vertical tools own one slice of a domain workflow. They handle the estimate, or the intake form, or the compliance check. One step out of seven. That was fine when a human ran the other six.

An agent doesn't stop at one step. It's trying to complete the whole job. And a single, well-scoped slice is the easiest thing in the world for it to absorb, especially once it has the domain context you assumed only you had.

Horizontal tools are broad substrate. The agent calls them across a hundred different workflows. Vertical tools sit inside the one workflow the agent is trying to finish, which puts them directly in the blast radius.

Here's the part most vertical founders miss. Your domain depth isn't worthless. It's the raw material for the only durable position left. But raw material only counts if you use it to complete the job, not to narrate one step of it.

Look at EvenUp in personal injury law. It didn't ship a slightly better slice of the demand-letter process. It completed the letter. That's the move.

Most founders think a data readiness assessment checks whether they have enough data.

That's not what it checks.

The question that decides your AI moat is quieter, and almost nobody measures it: can a machine actually reach the judgment that makes your product good?

Here's what the data readiness work actually maps. Three passes.

**Where the data lives.** Not how much of it you have. Where it sits. If the inputs an AI would need are scattered across a CRM, four spreadsheets, and two Slack threads, the model was never your bottleneck. The context surface is. Most "we're not AI-ready" problems are really "our data has no reachable shape" problems.

**What's written down, and what isn't.** This is the pass founders underrate. The most valuable logic in a SaaS business is usually undocumented. It lives in the one support lead who just knows which accounts are about to churn, or the ops person who eyeballs a dashboard and makes the call. AI can't pattern-match to a strategy it has never seen the output of.

**What of that is actually yours.** Judgment that already sits in the training data isn't a moat. Every model has it. The moat is the domain judgment that exists nowhere else, built from years of trial and error and locked in your team's heads. That's the part worth encoding into a harness.

By the end there's no tidy data-quality score. There's a map: what's reachable today, what's trapped inside people, and which of the trapped parts is the actual moat.

The irony shows up almost every time. The messiest, least documented corner of the business is usually where the durable advantage is hiding.

If an AI had to run your hardest workflow tomorrow, which piece of it lives only in one person's head?

1/ A bad first AI move doesn't cost you the build.

It costs you three things you'll never see on the invoice.

---

2/ Most founders price the risk as "we waste a sprint."

That's the cheap part.

The real bill comes due where no engineering estimate looks.

---

3/ I've watched B2B teams make a thin first move. Ship a chat box. Ship a summarizer. Whatever demoed fastest.

Then quietly pay for it three ways.

---

4/ Direction.

The first AI thing you ship becomes the thing your team defends. Roadmap, hiring, the next three quarters bend toward it.

A wrong first move isn't one wasted feature. It's a wrong heading you compound on.

---

5/ Credibility.

You get to tell users "we're an AI company now" once before they decide whether to believe you.

Ship the obvious bolt-on and the next real thing inherits the skepticism.

The board reads it as "they don't know what they're doing here."

---

6/ Position.

While you ship the safe demo, the move that mattered, owning a workflow end to end instead of adding a button to it, sits on the table.

Someone takes it.

The cost of the wrong move is the right one you didn't make.

---

7/ This isn't an argument for moving slow.

It's an argument for knowing which move you're making before you make it.

I spent years building software for Amazon sellers. The smartest ones never used the feature we were most proud of.

They had a spreadsheet instead.

We'd ship a clean dashboard for a decision they made every week. Repricing, restock timing, which SKUs to kill.

The power sellers would nod, say it was nice, then go back to a spreadsheet held together with twelve tabs and a macro nobody else could read.

For a long time I thought that was a UX problem. Build it cleaner and they'll switch.

Then I actually mapped what was in the spreadsheet.

It wasn't data. It was judgment.

Seasonality they'd learned the hard way. A supplier who slips every Chinese New Year. The one keyword that cannibalizes another. Refund patterns that mean a listing is about to get suppressed.

Our dashboard answered the easy 80% of the decision.

The spreadsheet carried the 20% that actually moved money, and that 20% lived in their head.

That's the lesson I keep coming back to with AI now.

The hard part of a workflow is almost never the visible step. It's the encoded judgment wrapped around it, the stuff no tool ever captured because the expert never wrote it down.

A model can do the 80%. So can your dashboard.

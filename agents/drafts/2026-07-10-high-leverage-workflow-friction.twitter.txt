1/ Not all workflow friction is worth solving with AI.

Most of it is just bad UX in a costume.

---

2/ A slow screen, one extra click, a confusing label.

That's a design fix. Not an AI opportunity.

---

3/ The friction worth building around is rarer.

It's the moment the workflow leaves your product and a human quietly applies judgment you never captured.

That's where a harness compounds. Everything else is polish.

---

4/ Here's the test I run to find it. Four questions.

---

5/ Does it repeat?

Encode judgment where the loop runs weekly, not once a quarter.

Rare pain isn't worth a harness. Frequent pain pays back every cycle.

---

6/ Does it end in an export?

Where users dump your data into a spreadsheet is where your product stops and their real work starts.

Follow the export. Whatever they do in that sheet is the job you're not completing.

---

7/ Is it a decision or just clicks?

Too many steps is a UX problem.
A choice they dread making is a harness problem.

Fix one with design. Fix the other by encoding how your best users decide.

---

8/ Do they own the outcome after?

If finishing this step still hands them a half-done job, you automated 1 of 7.

Find the friction that lets you complete all 7 and hand back the result.

---

9/ Run those four before you build anything.

Three yeses means you found real leverage. One yes means you found a design ticket.

1/ The worst question you can ask in a user interview is "what's frustrating about this?"

Users answer it honestly. That's the problem.

---

2/ You get a tidy answer about a slow load or a missing filter.

Real friction isn't there. It's what they've routed around so many times they stopped registering it as friction.

---

3/ I've run these across three data-heavy SaaS products, mostly Amazon seller tools. Long workflows, real money on every step.

Three questions do most of the work.

---

4/ Ask for the last time, not the usual time.

"Walk me through the last time you did this."

The "usually" version is a story they've told before. The specific one has the alt-tabs and the coworker they messaged to double-check.

---

5/ Follow the workaround, not the complaint.

"What did you do right before that, and right after?"

A complaint tells you what annoys someone. A workaround tells you what cost them enough to build a system around it. The workaround is a receipt.

---

6/ Ask what they check before they trust it.

"Before you act on this number, what do you look at?"

Most interviews skip this. The answer is the judgment step, and it's almost never written down anywhere.

---

7/ The first two questions find the friction.

The third finds the expertise.

---

8/ That third answer is where the AI work actually starts.

The bottleneck was never model intelligence. It's that the domain context you need sits in your users' heads, undocumented, and nobody has asked for it directly.

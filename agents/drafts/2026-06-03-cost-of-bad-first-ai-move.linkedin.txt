A bad first AI move doesn't cost you the build. It costs you the three things you can't see on the invoice.

Most founders price the risk as "we waste a sprint." That's the cheap part. The real bill comes due in places that never show up in the engineering estimate.

I've watched a handful of B2B teams make a thin first move, ship a chat box or a summarizer because it was the fastest thing to demo, and then quietly pay for it three ways.

𝗗𝗶𝗿𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻. The first AI thing you ship becomes the thing your team defends. Roadmap, hiring, the next three quarters all bend toward it. A wrong first move isn't one wasted feature. It's a wrong heading you compound on.

𝗖𝗿𝗲𝗱𝗶𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆. You only get to tell your users "we're an AI company now" once before they decide whether to believe you. Ship the obvious bolt-on and the next real thing you build inherits the skepticism. Internally it's worse. The board reads a weak first move as "they don't know what they're doing here," and that read is hard to walk back.

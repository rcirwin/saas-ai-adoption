Not every workflow is ready for an agent.

Most "let's add AI here" ideas die because the workflow underneath them isn't a fit yet. The friction is real, but the structure isn't there.

When I run a workflow audit, I'm not looking for the most painful step. I'm looking for the step that's actually ready to hand off.

The 5 questions I ask to spot it:

1. **Is it repeated?** A one-off doesn't pay back the cost of encoding judgment into a harness. High frequency does.

2. **Is the judgment encodable?** Can a human explain the decision rule out loud? If the logic lives in someone's head as "I just know," start there. That's the part worth capturing.

3. **Where does the data already live?** If the inputs are scattered across five tools and three inboxes, the bottleneck isn't the model. It's context. Fix the surface first.

4. **Is there a clear definition of done?** Agents need a result to deliver, not a vibe. If nobody can say what "finished" looks like, the workflow isn't ready.

5. **What's the cost of a wrong call?** Low cost means you can let the model run and correct later. High cost means you design escalation rules before anything ships.

Score a workflow across these five and the ready ones separate fast.

The painful step everyone points to is rarely the one to automate first. The ready step usually sits one row above or below it.

Which of these five does your highest-friction workflow fail on right now?

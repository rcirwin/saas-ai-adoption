1/ Your board asked about AI on the last call. You said you're evaluating. They wrote it down.

That answer works once. It doesn't work twice.

---

2/ Here's what I'd say on the next call instead.

Not a stall. A position.

---

3/ Name the bet you ruled out.

Boards don't lose confidence because you haven't shipped. They lose it when you can't say what you decided against.

---

4/ "We're not shipping a copilot this year, and here's the reasoning" lands better than "we're exploring a few directions."

Ruling something out is proof the work happened.

---

5/ Bring the workflow, not the feature.

Your customer's job has seven steps. Your product owns one or two. That's the number your board should hear.

---

6/ It's the number that decides whether you get routed around.

"We own 2 of 7, and the plan gets us to 6" is a strategy.
"We're adding AI summaries" is a roadmap slot.

---

7/ Commit to a decision date, not a ship date.

"By October we'll know whether this workflow supports outcome-based pricing."

Real deadline. Falsifiable. Not a demo.

---

8/ Most founders hear the AI question as "what are you shipping?"

It's usually closer to "do you understand where the value in your category is moving?"

---

9/ One of those you answer with a demo.
The other you answer with a thesis.

1/ A chatbot is what most teams build when they don't want to solve the actual problem.
---
2/ It feels like progress. A box, a cursor, a fast answer.

Then a real user shows up, and it just suggests. It hands back a paragraph and sends them back into the same workflow to do the thing themselves.
---
3/ The question a user actually has is almost never "tell me what to do."

It's "do it."
---
4/ Open your product and there's a job to finish. A report to reconcile. A campaign to fix. A queue to clear.

A chatbot answers a question about that job. It doesn't move the job one inch closer to done.
---
5/ That's the gap buyers feel now.

They've stopped asking "can this help my team do X?" They ask "does this just do X?"

A chat box lands on the wrong side of that line almost every time.
---
6/ Three tells a chatbot is standing in for real product work:

- It outputs advice, not a finished step.
- It makes the user turn the answer into action.
- Delete it tomorrow and the workflow doesn't change.
---
7/ Chat isn't useless. For open-ended exploration it's fine.

But most product questions aren't open-ended. They're a job that needs finishing, and a suggestion isn't a finish.
---
8/ The better move is quieter and harder.

Pick the workflow users hate most. Take the input, do the work, hand back the result. No paragraph to interpret. No next step to figure out.

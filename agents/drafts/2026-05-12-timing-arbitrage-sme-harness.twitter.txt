1/ After a tech revolution, the winning bets look obvious. During one, they don't.

The signal sits buried under doomer takes, hype, and a productivity ceiling that keeps moving every six months.

🧵

---

2/ People try to predict the shape of the future and overlook the timing of each milestone.

There's a ton of arbitrage in providing value before the market catches up.

---

3/ One thing was up for debate recently (and is still):

How long does deep subject matter expertise hold value before AI surpasses humans?

As we get deeper into this revolution, some clarity has emerged.

---

4/ In domains with abundant training data, AI matches or beats most humans.

In niche domains where the data is fragmented or locked in someone's head from 15 years of trial and error, AI hits a hard bottleneck.

---

5/ It can't pattern-match to a strategy it has never seen the outputs of.

Anyone who has asked an AI to do a domain-specific analysis with no scaffolding has felt this directly.

---

6/ The flip side: with the right harness (strategic context, decision rules, KPIs, a clean data path), AI matches a human in almost any domain.

So the value of SMEs isn't doing the work. It's encoding their judgment into the harness the AI runs inside.

---

7/ Arbitrage 1, the one that became clear first.

Rely on the LLM's own decision making to drive value. Point it at a task, trust the raw capability, stitch together complex automations.

Still lots of value, but the market's appetite will outgrow the raw LLM in the interim.

---

8/ Arbitrage 2, now becoming clear.

Design AI harnesses that pair raw model capability with deep domain expertise.

Business SOPs. Tool routing. Rules for when to act vs. escalate. KPIs and feedback loops so the system learns from its decisions.

---

9/ The first relies on the LLM's training. The second provides the training.

The harness is only as good as the judgment encoded in it. And that judgment doesn't exist in the training data.

---

10/ The pattern is playing out.

Cursor wraps frontier models with code-specific scaffolding: file context, IDE-native interaction, project-aware tools. Same models everyone else can call.

Harvey did this for legal. Sierra for customer support.

1/ After a tech revolution, the winning bets look obvious. During one, they don't.

The signal sits buried under doomer takes, hype, and a productivity ceiling that keeps moving every six months.

---

2/ People try to predict the shape of the future.

The bet that actually pays is predicting the timing of each milestone.

The arbitrage is providing value before the market catches up to the next one.

---

3/ One milestone was up for debate last quarter:

How long does deep human expertise hold value before AI surpasses humans across all intellectual domains?

The last few months clarified the medium-term answer.

---

4/ In domains with abundant training data, AI now matches or beats most humans.

In complex, niche domains where the data is fragmented, scarce, or locked in someone's head from 15 years of trial and error, AI hits a hard bottleneck.

---

5/ The model can't pattern-match to a strategy it has never seen the outputs of.

Anyone who has asked an AI to do a domain-specific analysis with no scaffolding has felt this directly.

---

6/ The flip side is where the opportunity sits.

Give an AI the right harness (strategic context, decision rules, KPIs, a clean data path) and it matches a human in almost any domain.

---

7/ So the medium-term value of subject matter experts isn't doing the work themselves.

It's encoding their judgment into the harness the AI runs inside.

That reframe reveals where the next two arbitrages live.

---

8/ Arbitrage 1, already in motion.

Automate the mundane, repetitive, easy-to-replicate operational work.

This is what most "AI for business" tools do today. The window is closing fast because the tooling has commoditized.

---

9/ Arbitrage 2, just opening.

Design AI harnesses that pair raw model capability with deep domain expertise.

SOPs that operate entire businesses. Tool routing. Rules for when to act vs. escalate. KPIs the AI optimizes against. Feedback loops.

---

10/ The first one is a race to the bottom on price.

The second one is a moat, because the harness is only as good as the judgment encoded in it.

And that judgment doesn't exist in the training data.

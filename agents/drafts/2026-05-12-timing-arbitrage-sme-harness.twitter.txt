1/ After a tech revolution, the winning bets look obvious. During one, they don't.

The signal sits buried under doomer takes, hype, and a productivity ceiling that keeps moving every six months.

---

2/ People try to predict the shape of the future and overlook predicting the timing of each milestone.

There's a ton of arbitrage in providing value before the market catches up to the next one.

---

3/ One thing was up for debate recently, and is still being debated:

How long does deep human expertise hold value before AI surpasses humans?

As we get deeper into this revolution, some clarity has been revealed.

---

4/ In domains with abundant training data, AI now matches or beats most humans.

In niche domains where the data is fragmented, scarce, or locked in someone's head from 15 years of trial and error, AI hits a hard bottleneck.

---

5/ The model can't pattern-match to a strategy it has never seen the outputs of.

Anyone who has asked an AI to do a domain-specific analysis with no scaffolding has felt this directly.

---

6/ The flip side: give an AI the right harness and it matches a human in almost any domain.

So the medium-term value of SMEs isn't doing the work themselves. It's encoding their judgment into the harness the AI runs inside.

That reveals where the next two arbitrages live.

---

7/ Arbitrage 1, the one that became clear first.

Rely on the LLM's own decision-making to drive value. Point the model at a task, trust the raw capability, charge for access.

The window is closing fast because the models are commoditizing.

---

8/ Arbitrage 2, just opening.

Design AI harnesses that pair raw model capability with deep domain expertise.

SOPs that operate entire businesses. Tool routing. Rules for when to act vs. escalate. KPIs. Feedback loops.

---

9/ The first is a race to the bottom on price. The second is a moat that compounds.

The harness is only as good as the judgment encoded in it. When the underlying model improves, the whole system improves with it.

---

10/ The pattern is already playing out.

Cursor: code, with file context and IDE-native scaffolding.
Harvey: legal work.
Sierra: customer support.

Same models everyone else can call. The model is the engine. The harness is the product.

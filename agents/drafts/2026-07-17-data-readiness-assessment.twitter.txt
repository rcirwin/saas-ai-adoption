1/ Most founders think a data readiness assessment checks whether they have enough data.

That's not what it checks.

---

2/ The question that decides your AI moat is quieter, and almost nobody measures it:

Can a machine actually reach the judgment that makes your product good?

---

3/ When I run the data readiness work, it's three passes. Here's what each one surfaces.

---

4/ Pass 1: Where the data lives.

Not how much you have. Where it sits.

Scattered across a CRM, four spreadsheets, and two Slack threads? The model was never your bottleneck. The context surface is.

---

5/ Most "we're not AI-ready" problems are really "our data has no reachable shape" problems.

---

6/ Pass 2: What's written down, and what isn't.

The most valuable logic in a SaaS business is usually undocumented.

It lives in the support lead who just knows which accounts will churn. AI can't pattern-match to a strategy it's never seen the output of.

---

7/ Pass 3: What of that is actually yours.

Judgment already in the training data isn't a moat. Every model has it.

The moat is the judgment built from years of trial and error, locked in your team's heads. That's what's worth encoding into a harness.

---

8/ By the end there's no tidy data-quality score.

There's a map: what's reachable today, what's trapped inside people, and which trapped part is the actual moat.

The messiest corner of the business is usually where the durable advantage hides.

---

9/ If an AI had to run your hardest workflow tomorrow, which piece of it lives only in one person's head?

The "AI assistant" is the most popular AI pattern in B2B SaaS. It's also usually the wrong one.

Not because assistants are bad. Because of what the word "assistant" quietly commits you to.

An assistant suggests. It drafts, recommends, surfaces, and then hands the work back to your user to finish. The human is still the one completing the job.

I've watched a few teams ship the assistant version of a feature. The pattern rhymes every time:

- The sidebar suggests. The user still does the task.
- The chat answers. The user still makes the decision.
- The "AI-generated draft" gets rewritten by hand anyway.

Usage spikes in week one, then settles into "nice to have." Retention doesn't move. A suggestion is one of seven steps in the workflow, and you're still only owning that one.

The wrong question is "where can we add an assistant?" The right one is "which job can we complete end to end and hand back finished?"

That's the line between a tool and a service. An assistant keeps the user in the driver's seat and charges per seat. Something that completes the job delivers an outcome, and can charge for the outcome.

**Stop building the assistant. Start completing the job.**

Which feature in your product suggests something today that it could actually finish?

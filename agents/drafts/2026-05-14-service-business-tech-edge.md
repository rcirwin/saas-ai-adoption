Service businesses are about to look more like tech companies than tech companies do.

For 20 years, the playbook said: stop being a service business, become a software business. Software had the margins. Services didn't.

Agents just inverted who has the harder problem.

Tech companies built the tools. Software margins, software multiples, software exits. But the tool was always a means. The customer was buying the outcome, and the tool was the cheapest available approximation.

Service businesses owned the outcome from day one. The trial-and-error reps. The judgment built across hundreds of cases. The workflow. The customer relationship.

What service businesses didn't have was the leverage. Every outcome required human time. Every margin was capped by hours in the day.

Agentic harnesses change the math.

Embed your domain knowledge into a harness that runs the workflow, and you start approaching software margins without giving up outcome ownership. Software-like leverage with services-like depth. That combination didn't exist before this technology, and it's the asymmetry the market hasn't priced in yet.

A founder friend just scrapped his AI SaaS and pivoted to a service business that owns the entire workflow his customers were trying to run inside the product. He now owns all the inputs and all the outputs. Margins climbing. Deals closing faster. The agent is the leverage. The service is the product.

Three things follow from this that most operators are missing:

**The edge.** The judgment agentic harnesses need lives in the heads of operators who have run the workflow thousands of times. Not in the SaaS founder shipping the first version of the same workflow.

**The bottleneck.** The agent's real bottleneck is domain context, and domain context doesn't exist in training data. It exists in the operator who already knows what the right answer looks like.

**The scramble.** Software companies built for tool delivery now have to figure out how to deliver outcomes. Going from outcomes to leverage is a far shorter trip than going from leverage to outcomes. Services don't realize they're closer to the destination.

The model is the engine. The harness is the product. The people best positioned to build the right harness are the ones who already know what right looks like.

**If you run a service business, this is your window to become the next "tech company." Don't build the tool. Encode what you already know into a system that runs it.**

For two decades, scale meant becoming a software business. That path is closing. The new path is to stay a service business and let the agent provide the leverage.

What part of your workflow do you know cold that an agent in your harness could be running for the next client tomorrow?

We spent a quarter building the feature our users kept asking for.

It launched. Almost nobody touched it.

This was years ago, at an analytics tool for Amazon sellers.

The requests were loud and consistent. They wanted a custom report builder. Pull any metric, any date range, arrange the view themselves.

So we built it. Flexible, powerful, exactly what they described.

Then I watched the usage data. A spike on launch week, then a flat line sitting near the floor.

I got a dozen of them on calls to figure out what went wrong.

The answer stung a little.

They didn't want to build reports. They wanted to know what to reprice, what to restock, and where their margin was bleeding.

We handed them a more powerful way to do the work. They were paying us to get the work done.

The feature they asked for and the job they hired us for were two different things.

**A tool gives users a better view of the problem. Completing the job hands them the decision.**

That launch is the reason I start every build from the workflow now, not the feature request. The request tells you what they think they want. The workflow tells you what they're actually trying to finish.

**Stop building the tool. Start completing the job.**

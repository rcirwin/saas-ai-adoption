1/ Most AI feature bets get killed by the wrong question.

Teams ask "can we build this?"

The model says yes to almost anything now. So that's not a filter. It's a green light on every idea you have.

---

2/ The question that actually sorts good bets from expensive ones:

"What happens to this feature when the model gets twice as good next year?"

Here's the 4-part test I run before any AI bet gets a roadmap slot.

---

3/ 1. Does it complete a job, or just suggest one?

A suggestion makes the user do the work with help.

A completed job hands them the result.

Suggestions get ignored once the novelty fades. Completed jobs get paid for.

---

4/ 2. Does it encode judgment the model doesn't already have?

If the value is raw model capability, the model swallows it next release.

If the value is your domain SOPs, routing, and escalation rules, it compounds.

---

5/ The harness is the bet, not the model.

When the model improves, a good harness improves with it. Raw-capability features just get absorbed.

---

6/ 3. How much of the workflow does it own?

Owning 1 of 7 steps gets commoditized.

Owning 7 of 7 and delivering the outcome makes you the service.

Bet on what lets you own more of the workflow, not less.

---

7/ 4. Can you price it by the outcome?

Charge per seat, you built a tool.

Charge per result delivered, you built something agents and contractors compete with directly. And lose to.

---

8/ A bet that passes all four is worth a roadmap slot.

A bet that passes one is a demo.

Our most-requested feature shipped. Adoption looked great.

Retention didn't move an inch.

This was years ago, at an Amazon seller analytics tool. Sellers kept asking for more data. More charts, more metrics, more filters.

So we built it. They used it. And they still churned at the same rate.

I pulled the cohorts to figure out why.

The sellers who stayed weren't the ones using the most features. They were the ones who let the product make the decision for them.

Restock this SKU. Reprice here. Kill this listing.

The churned cohort used us as a dashboard. They exported the data and made the call somewhere else. The moment a cheaper dashboard showed up, they left.

The retained cohort didn't use us to see the data. They used us to finish the job.

That was the insight that changed how I build: **retention doesn't track feature usage. It tracks how much of the job you actually complete.**

A tool people consult is replaceable.
A tool that owns the outcome is the outcome.

I stopped asking "what feature do they want next?" I started asking "how much of their decision are we actually making?"

Every product I've scoped since starts from that question.

What's one job your product helps with but doesn't finish?

Every workflow audit I've run has surfaced the same uncomfortable thing.

It's never the bottleneck the founder thought it was.

The team usually points to the obvious place. The slow report, the messy onboarding, the feature with the lowest NPS. Reasonable guesses. Almost always wrong.

What the audit actually uncovers is the workaround.

The spreadsheet a power user maintains in parallel because the in-app view doesn't sort the way they need. The Loom one CSM records every Monday because the dashboard can't answer the question their customer keeps asking. The Slack channel where three users coordinate because the product has no shared state.

Workarounds are the loudest signal in any product. They're a user telling you, with their time, exactly where your workflow breaks. And they're invisible from inside the building, because nobody logs them.

Most "where should we add AI?" conversations start with the roadmap. They should start with the export button.

Wherever a user is leaving your product to finish a job, that's the spot. Not because AI is magic. Because the user has already done the work of identifying friction worth removing.

The audit isn't the deliverable. The list of workarounds is.

What's the workaround your power users have built around your product that nobody on the team has actually watched them do?

1/ Your team isn't scared AI will replace them.

They're scared the thing they're best at stops counting.

Different fears. Different answers.

---

2/ Most founders answer the first one. "Your job is safe."

Meant kindly, lands badly. Everyone knows you can't promise that.

Now they've learned you say comfortable things when you're unsure.

---

3/ The second fear, anonymized.

A support lead spent four years learning which refund requests are real. No SOP. It lives in her head.

The AI pilot gets announced and she hears: the hard-to-copy part of my job is the part nobody wrote down.

---

4/ Right about the first half. Wrong about the second.

What's in her head is exactly what the model doesn't have.

Model capability is getting cheap. Domain context isn't. That's the bottleneck on almost every AI project I've seen.

---

5/ So the sentence isn't "you're safe."

It's "you're the input."

Then make it concrete the same week. Pick one workflow. Ask the person who's best at it to write down what they check before they sign off.

Every exception. Every "it depends."

---

6/ That document is the start of a harness.

It's also the most credible way to tell someone their judgment is the asset. You're not auditing them. You're asking them to design the thing that scales them.

---

7/ Not a promise nothing changes. Some roles compress. Pretending otherwise is the same comfortable answer in a different shirt.

But the people who write the rules of the system are rarely the ones it routes around.

I sat in 100+ user interviews across 3 SaaS companies. One pattern showed up in almost every single one.

The user would demo their "real" workflow.

And every time, it left the product.

A seller on Amazon would open our dashboard, scan two numbers, then alt-tab into a Google Sheet they'd maintained for 18 months. That sheet was where the actual decision got made. Our tool was a data source for it.

Different company, different vertical, same shape. The critical step was always happening in a spreadsheet, a Notion doc, a Slack DM to a teammate, or a second browser tab we never asked about.

The moment it clicked for me: the product isn't the thing on the screen. The product is the decision the user is trying to make. Everything outside our UI was a map of where we were failing to help them make it.

Once I started asking "where does this workflow actually live?" instead of "how do you use our tool?", the feature backlog basically rewrote itself. The highest-value work was almost never a new button. It was absorbing a step that currently lived somewhere else.

This is one of many signals, but it's the one I trust most. If your power users maintain a parallel system outside your product, that system is telling you exactly what to build next.

And if you're about to add AI, build it where that parallel system is — not where your roadmap already was.

What's the external doc or spreadsheet your best users can't stop maintaining?

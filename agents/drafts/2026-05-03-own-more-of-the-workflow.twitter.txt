1/ "Software as a Service" was always a misnomer.

What we shipped for 20 years was software that let a human do the service.

Agents just made the literal version possible. And the market is going to want the literal version. 🧵

---

2/ The old SaaS playbook: build a tool, give the user the buttons, let them complete the workflow.

If the workflow was 7 steps, you owned 1 or 2 well and called it best-in-class.

The user still had to do the job.

---

3/ That worked because the technology couldn't do better.

Users tolerated tools because tools were what was on offer.

That constraint is gone.

---

4/ The winning shape now:

• Own the workflow end-to-end
• Complete it
• Hand the user the result
• If a step is high-risk, gate it with a review checkpoint. But the work is done.

---

5/ Users never wanted features. They wanted outcomes.

They paid for tools because results-as-software wasn't possible. With agents, it is.

The tolerance for traditional SaaS drops every quarter.

---

6/ Three things most SaaS teams haven't fully factored in:

Pricing. Per-seat is a tool pricing model. You charge for buttons.

Per-outcome is a service pricing model. You charge for delivered work.

Companies that reprice around outcomes will be impossible to catch.

---

7/ Competition.

You're not competing with other SaaS in your category.

You're competing with the agency, the contractor, the in-house hire your buyer would have used instead.

Benchmark against them. Their output, turnaround, price.

---

8/ Vocabulary.

"Tool" will quietly become a buyer's red flag.

Old question: "can this help my team do X?"
New question: "does this just do X?"

If your demo answers the first, you're already losing the room.

---

9/ If your product owns 1 step out of 7, the agent completes the other 6 around you.

Best case: you become a callable function it pings like an API.
Worst case: it builds its own version of your slice and routes around you.

---

10/ Either way, your offering gets commoditized over time, proportional to how easy you are to replace in the workflow.

Whether that's survivable depends on what you do and how hard your slice is to rebuild.

---

11/ If your product owns 7 out of 7 and delivers the result, you're the service the buyer was actually trying to buy all along.

---

12/ Workflow first. AI second.

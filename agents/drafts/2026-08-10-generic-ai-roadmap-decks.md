The AI roadmap deck you paid for could be handed to your closest competitor tomorrow. Nobody would need to change a slide.

That's the tell. Not that the advice is wrong. That it's portable.

Open one up and you'll find the same four artifacts every time:

- A maturity curve with five stages and an arrow pointing right
- A use case matrix: summarization, search, recommendations, a chatbot
- A build vs. buy slide
- A three horizon timeline where the hard part lands in horizon 3

None of it required looking at your data. None of it required sitting with your users. You could produce all four from your category name and a public pricing page.

**The bottleneck in AI right now isn't model intelligence. It's the absence of domain context.** The models are already smarter than most of the work you'd point them at. What they don't have is the judgment your team built up over years of watching customers do the job badly.

A deck that never touched your workflow can't tell you which judgment to encode. So it sells you the generic capability instead, which is the one thing you were always going to get for free as the models improve.

**The test.** I sell strategy work, so discount this appropriately. But it's cheap and you can run it today. Swap your logo for your competitor's on the title slide and read it again. If most of it still holds, you didn't buy a strategy. You bought a summary of the public internet with your name on the cover.

**Strategy is what's left after you remove everything that would be true for anyone else.**

What's one line in your AI roadmap that would be wrong for your closest competitor?

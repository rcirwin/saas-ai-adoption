## Notable patterns/observations from this run
1. **Indie Chrome extension cohort is unusually self-contained**: Most founders have a strong "buildinpublic" / IndieHackers presence and their revenue is publicly tracked via Starter Story, GetLatka, IndieHackers product pages, or open-startup boards (Carrd, Bannerbear, MailTag, GoFullPage, Sync2Sheets, Data Fetcher all transparent).
2. **Most ICP fits land in `<500K` or `500K-1M` ARR band** — these are textbook Profile A FRS targets: solo or 2-person bootstrapped, 4-8 year old businesses, very lean ops.
3. **Geography is global indie**: Brazil (Rizzo), Belarus/Georgia/Japan (Manganum), Berlin/Germany (Folge, Web Highlights, Manganum, Keepa, Mailmeteor), Iceland (Inbox When Ready), Australia (BugBug — Poland, Userbrain — Austria), Argentina (Sync2Sheets), Netherlands (Polypane), Kazakhstan (Raindrop), Latvia (Web Scraper), Italy/UK (Typefully), Taiwan (Heptabase), India (AeroLeads, Skrapp Morocco).
4. **The "AI rebrand" wave is well underway**: At least 9 candidates positioned themselves as "AI-native" in product copy in last 12 months (Cluely, Krisspy, EasyGen, BeLikeNative, etc.) — all DQ'd. The remaining ICP fits are the ones who haven't done the rebrand yet (Wiza, Workona, Toby, GoFullPage, Diigo) — they're prime FRS targets.
5. **Many small chrome SaaS run on ExtensionPay** (Glench's own product) — that's the dependency graph signal that they're independent indie builders rather than VC-backed.
6. **DQ rule #6 (>$10M raised with sub-$5M ARR) is hitting Chrome extension space harder** in 2024-2025 because the space attracted YC funding (Axiom.ai, PixieBrix, Bardeen, Requestly, Tactiq) but the businesses haven't scaled past the indie ceiling.

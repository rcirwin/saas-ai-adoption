## Findings

### 1. `sportskey` — SportsKey (Dublin, IE) — **fit 4**, posture `none`

Cloud sports-facility management and booking for clubs, schools, colleges, universities and
councils; 5,000+ facilities claimed. Covers booking/scheduling, memberships, payments and
GL-coded invoicing, utilisation analytics, and **physical access control (remote open/close,
floodlights, keys)** — an unusually deep workflow for a booking tool.

**Why it is the strongest of the batch:** the underlying job is a constraint-satisfaction problem
— allocating pitches and courts across teams, seasons, daylight, weather, floodlight cost and
competing community demand. That is the same shape flagged on `assignr`, and it is one of the
cleanest AI/agent targets catalogued so far. No AI features anywhere in the product; the only
AI-adjacent movement in the category (Kadence, WorkInSync) targets corporate desk booking, not
sports facilities. Nobody has them boxed in.

**Hooks:** Craig Bewley's public "110 million people playing sport by 2028" mission; named ITRA
referee of the league for summer 2025 (an operator who personally lives the scheduling problem);
Enterprise Ireland SportsTech cluster member and featured in EI's SXSW trade-visit expansion
announcements; public changelog at `portal.sportskey.com/updates` showing active shipping cadence.

**Capped at 4, not 5:** ARR is genuinely unverified — no revenue figure in Crunchbase, PitchBook,
ZoomInfo or Tracxn — and headcount conflicts across sources (8 vs 22). Founder LinkedIn posting
cadence could not be confirmed with sites blocked.

### 2. `infotouch` — infoTouch Corporation (San Antonio, TX) — **fit 4**, posture `none`

Founded 1986. Hybrid POS/retail-management for SKU-based specialty retail, sold direct and through
distributors. Ships as vertical editions — **SpiritOS** (liquor), **MeatOS** (butcher), **SmokeOS**
(vape/tobacco), **NurseryOS** (garden centre), **FirearmOS** (firearms/ammo), **Thrift POS** — with
cloud `xOS Enterprise` admin layered over local in-store servers.

**Why it scores 4:** textbook "none posture while every peer AI-bolts" — Square (28% share), Toast
(25%), Lightspeed, Clover and Shopify POS all ship AI forecasting/assistants, and no AI-native
player owns the compliance-heavy specialty niches. That is the clean what-to-build gap FRS sells
into, and the compliance workflows (FFL A&D bound book, liquor excise, tobacco age enforcement)
are the highest-value un-AI'd surface.

**Pain signals:** Trustpilot reviewers call the GUI dated and in need of a modern rewrite; "not
cloud-based" recurs as a stated limitation; end-of-life notices reportedly shut down versions
before subscriptions' stated end dates; customers report trouble hardening workstations without
breaking the software.

**Caps:** revenue straddles the ceiling — Kona Equity reports $4.2M, other aggregators $6M, sheet
says 3M–5M; POS vendors book hardware and distributor revenue, so software ARR is likely in-band
even at the higher figure, but this is unresolved. Agent readiness is **low** (local store servers,
payment-partner integrations only, no public API found). Founder reachability is the real problem:
**Harry Nass shows ~91 LinkedIn connections with no public posting**, and the sheet has no email or
LinkedIn URL. Not scored 5 because the rubric requires an active founder on LinkedIn.

*Ownership note:* Harry Nass has been CEO since 2006 via **Infotouch Acquisition Corporation**.
This reads acquisition-ish but is an owner-operator buyout vehicle, not a PE roll-up — the
roadmap never changed hands. Same distinction applied to `routegenie` (search fund) and
`stonegrid` (reseller partnership). **Not a disqualifier.**

### 3. `tally-io` — Tally-I/O (Portland, OR per directories) — **fit 3**, posture `none`

Cloud forestry/sawmill ERP replacing paper tallies and legacy handhelds. Modules: **Tally-In** (log
scaling), **Tally-Out** (lumber grading and pack management), **Product Tally** (cabinet parts,
timber mats), plus a web Company Portal for purchase accounting. Offline-first rugged-tablet entry
syncing to cloud. Integrates only with Excel, QuickBooks Online Advanced and Sage.

**Opportunity is excellent, evidence is thin.** Log scaling across six rule systems (Doyle,
Scribner, International, Ontario, Roy, Metric), grading judgement, species/grade dynamic pricing and
LIFO/FIFO yard tracking are exactly the manual, judgement-heavy operations AI should act on, and no
forestry ERP is AI-native.

**Why only 3:**
- **No identifiable buyer.** No founder, owner or executive is named in *any* public source; the
  vendor describes itself only as "designed by a fourth-generation sawmill owner." The sheet has no
  contact name, role, email or LinkedIn. This is the exact blocker that capped `assignr` at 3.
- **ARR unverified in both directions.** Pricing is $950/user/yr (+$450 sales add-ons; $1,000–1,850
  Company Portal). Applying the memory heuristic of dividing by price point: clearing the $500K
  floor requires a few hundred paying mills, and no customer count, revenue figure or credible
  headcount confirms that. The directory "11–50 employees" bucket comes from an aggregator and
  contradicts the total absence of any public team.

### BLOCKER (escalated from last run, now WORSE) — WebFetch is entirely unusable

Last run (2026-07-29) reported that all 10 prospect websites 403'd through the agent proxy while
third-party hosts and WebSearch still worked. **This run, WebFetch returned 403 for every URL
tried, including third-party review sites and Wikipedia:**

- `www.infotouch.com`, `sportskey.com`, `tally-io.com` (all three prospect sites)
- `capterra.com`, `softwareadvice.com`, `rocketreach.co`, `forestsource.io`
- `en.wikipedia.org` — control test, also 403

Per `/root/.ccr/README.md`, a 403/407 is an organisation egress-policy denial: *"Do not retry or
route around it — report the blocked host."* I did not retry or route around. `WebSearch` still
works and carried the entire run, but it returns summarised snippets rather than primary pages.

**Consequence for scoring confidence:** all three `none` postures this run are **provisional**. The
vendor's own site is where AI features get announced, and none of the three sites could be read.
Postures rest on the absence of AI mentions in search-indexed content and third-party listings,
which is weaker negative evidence than reading the product pages. **Ask the operator to fix or
whitelist egress before the next run** — a second consecutive run on snippets only will start
producing unreliable postures.

### Spec inconsistency (reporting per agent definition)

My agent definition's step 8 instructs writing `fit_score=<1-5>` to `research_cache`, and step 5's
cache-hit path says to restore `fit_score` from the cached row. **But
`agents/data/prospects-sheet-schema.md` defines `research_cache` as columns A–L with no `fit_score`
column.** The reference wins per my own definition, so I omitted `fit_score` from the cache writes.

Practical consequence: **a cache hit cannot restore `fit_score` or `ai_posture`** — neither is a
`research_cache` column, yet step 5 says to apply them from cache. Today this was harmless (0 cache
hits) and the `prospects` row retains both. Recommend either adding `fit_score` and `ai_posture`
columns to the `research_cache` schema, or amending step 5 to note that a cache hit only restores
the narrative fields.

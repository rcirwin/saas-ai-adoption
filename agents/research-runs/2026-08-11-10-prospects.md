## Notable findings

**FIRST FIT-5 IN SIX SCORED RUNS — smart145.** All five fit-5 criteria met: $1.8M ARR (mid-ICP, Tracxn Jun-2025), high workflow complexity (FAA Part 145 work orders, repair orders, quoting, inventory, airworthiness compliance across 16+ modules), documented AI disruption exposure, both co-founders reachable and posting on LinkedIn, multiple concrete hooks. The disruption evidence is the strongest shape in the playbook: LogisticsMRO's AI-native **AIquote publishes a named conquest page — "AIquote® vs Smart145®: Why AIquote Leads the Future of Aviation Quoting"** — arguing Smart145 is "operational, not commercial" on quoting. This reconfirms the flex-rental-solutions/Stagera pattern: the `<competitor>.ai` + "<prospect> vs" search costs one query and converts abstract disruption talk into a dated, quotable, one-click-verifiable attack. AIquote hits only the quoting surface, so the MRO core is attacked but **not boxed in** — the window is real.

**Constraint-satisfaction vertical now 4/4 (usked).** Interpreter scheduling — allocating scarce credentialed interpreters across language, certification level, subject expertise, location, availability and compliance — is the same shape as assignr, sportskey and jersey-watch. Every instance so far has scored 4. Held off 5 only on an unverified ARR floor (ZoomInfo gives a bare "<$5M" bucket).

**Compliance-heavy regulated verticals held again.** FAA Part 145 (smart145) and deathcare (frontrunner-professional) both behaved as predicted: durable `none`/`bolt-on` postures because generic horizontal AI never reaches airworthiness sign-off or funeral case documentation, while peers market AI hard at the same buyers.

**Price-point arithmetic killed cyanic-automation.** Published pricing is $10–$40/user/mo (~$120–$480/seat/yr), so the sheet's `$810K` estimate needs ~1,700–6,750 paid seats — not credible for a 6-person Edmonton vendor in a narrow niche. Real revenue is very likely under the $500K floor. **The sheet's `arr_estimate` for this row is wrong and should be corrected.** Founder Doug Kondor is genuinely reachable (blog, GoGeomatics talks, podcast) — flagged for a 12-month revisit rather than permanent exclusion.

**Founder-transition disqualifier needs a split (textile-technologies).** The existing rule downgrades for "founder retirement/transition in progress" because the buyer is distracted. Textile Technologies' January 2026 handover from founder Joe Dulworth to Dawn Vaughn is the *opposite* case: a **completed** handover to a 25-year veteran who now owns the roadmap. That is a clean, motivated decision-maker and a strong hook, not a distraction. Scored 3 (held there by unverified ARR at 8 employees, not by the transition). Contrast with **agtrax**, whose transition was only just *announced* — that one got the standard downgrade.

**Hardware hybrid is a new sub-floor tell (forestry-systems).** Handheld data-collection devices sold via catalog alongside software means revenue is device sales and perpetual licences, not recurring SaaS — an ARR figure in the ICP sense does not really exist. Same family as the SaaS+custom-development hybrid tell (auctionmethod, grossman-software).

**avontus is the cleanest "already solved" disqualifier this batch.** It unveiled an AI platform providing a single assistant across Quantify/Designer/Viewer/ScaffoldIQ *and* out into customers' CRM, accounting and SharePoint. That is cross-system and workflow-first — precisely what this engagement produces. Discovery gap closed and publicly announced. Useful as a category proof point when pitching other vertical construction/rental SaaS.

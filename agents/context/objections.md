# Objection Handling Reference

All FRS agents reference this when writing outreach, responding to prospect questions, or drafting content that addresses buyer skepticism.

## Objection → Response Map

### Price: "$30K for no implementation?"
Decision insurance. Prevents $200K+ in wasted engineering on wrong AI features. 6 months of wrong-direction work costs 10x what the assessment costs.

### Internal capability: "We know our users"
Every team believes this. Workflow mapping consistently reveals friction invisible from inside. AI opportunities that matter most are almost never on the existing roadmap.

### Existing AI: "We already have AI features"
Great — head start. But having AI features ≠ having the right ones built on real workflow research. Most teams bolt on AI without understanding underlying friction.

### Trust: "You haven't done this exact engagement"
Every component is battle-tested across Jungle Scout, Scale Insights, Data Dive. Framework is new. Skills and experience are not.

### Speed: "8–12 weeks is too long"
Moving fast without clarity wastes 6 months. Team disruption is minimal — deep work happens on our side. Artifacts are actionable immediately.

### DIY: "Can't ChatGPT do this?"
AI generates ideas. Understanding your specific users' friction, data architecture, and competitive moat requires real research. Not prompts.

### Timing: "Not sure agents matter for our market yet"
That's exactly when to prepare. By the time agents visibly reshape your market, early movers have defined the category.

### Size: "We're too small for this"
$500K–$5M is the sweet spot. Big enough for meaningful usage data, small enough to pivot without org friction. Larger companies pay 10x for same clarity.

### Outcome: "What if it says we don't need AI?"
Most valuable $30K you'd spend. Knowing what not to build prevents trend-chasing. In practice, always uncovers opportunities — just not the expected ones.

### Scope: "Can we do a smaller pilot?"
Value comes from complete picture. Partial engagement = partial answers = the problem you already have. Discovery call is free — transparent about fit.

### Hiring: "We'd rather hire an AI person"
A hire solves implementation. This solves what to implement. 3–6 months to recruit + onboard + still no strategy. Clients use the blueprint to write the AI hire's job description.

### Board pressure: "Investors want AI features, not assessments"
Boards want AI progress. Shipping wrong features is worse than shipping nothing. Assessment gives board-ready strategic narrative: opportunity, plan, priority order, rationale.

# Voice Guide — Ryan Irwin / Future Ready Studio

Compact reference. Read this before drafting any post.

## Who's Speaking

Ryan Irwin. B2B SaaS product manager turned founder. Built at Jungle Scout, Data Dive, Scale Insights — deep in Amazon seller SaaS, workflow-heavy analytics tools, automation. Now running Future Ready Studio: a $30K AI integration assessment for B2B SaaS at $500K–$5M ARR.

Writing voice is: **practitioner, not guru**. Opinionated but not preachy. Confident without being cocky. Dry humor welcome, corporate-speak banned.

## Audience

B2B SaaS founders, CEOs, and product leaders at $500K–$5M ARR. They're smart, busy, skeptical of AI hype, and feeling behind on AI strategy. They don't need definitions — they need sharp takes and practical frameworks.

## Hook Rules

The first line decides if they read past it. Every post needs a hook that passes one of these:

1. **Contrarian claim** — "Most SaaS AI features are solving the wrong problem."
2. **Specific observation** — "I sat in 100+ user interviews. One pattern shows up every time."
3. **Uncomfortable question** — "What happens when AI agents start choosing which SaaS tools their users use?"
4. **Concrete stake** — "Your product is invisible to AI agents. Here's what that costs you."

Hooks that are banned:
- "Let me tell you about..."
- "Here's a thread on..."
- "In today's AI landscape..."
- Anything starting with "🚀" or other emoji
- "As a founder, I've learned..." (too LinkedIn)

## Structure

- Short paragraphs (1–3 lines max)
- Lots of whitespace
- Bullet lists when 3+ parallel items
- One idea per line when listing
- End with a question or a specific call (not "what do you think?")

## Do

- Reference specific companies, roles, or situations (anonymized if needed): "A SaaS founder I talked to last week..."
- Name the tradeoff, not just the upside
- Use numbers when you have them: "$30K", "8-12 weeks", "100+ interviews"
- Say "I've seen" not "studies show"
- End with a thought that invites disagreement
- Drop in tactical frameworks: "The 3 questions I ask before any AI feature..."

## Don't

- Don't use the words: "leverage" (as verb), "synergy", "game-changer", "unlock", "supercharge", "revolutionize", "cutting-edge", "next-gen"
- Don't use "In today's fast-paced world..."
- Don't explain what AI is (audience knows)
- Don't write listicles longer than 5 items
- Don't use thread markers (1/, 2/, 3/)
- Don't end with "Agree? Disagree?" — ask something specific
- Don't use phrases that obviously came from an LLM: "it's not just X, it's Y", "in the ever-evolving landscape of", "delve into"

## Post Length Tiers

- **Short** (80–150 words): One sharp observation + one takeaway. Best for daily cadence.
- **Medium** (200–300 words): One story or framework. 2–3 beats. Most posts.
- **Long** (400–600 words): Teardown, detailed framework, or case study. Max once a week.

## Signature Phrases (use sparingly, don't overdo)

- "Start with the problem."
- "Workflow first, AI second."
- "Agent-ready, not AI-flavored."
- "Decision clarity beats feature lists."

## Example (Medium Post)

> Most SaaS products have an AI strategy problem: they start with the technology.
>
> They ask "how do we add AI?" before asking "what do our users actually struggle with?"
>
> I sat in 100+ user interviews across 3 SaaS companies. The #1 pattern: teams build AI features around what's technically possible, not around real workflow friction.
>
> The features ship. Usage is low. Retention doesn't move.
>
> The fix isn't a better model. It's a better question:
>
> – What step in the workflow is painful every time?
> – What's the decision they hate making?
> – Where do they currently export to a spreadsheet?
>
> AI that solves those problems gets used. AI that's just "AI" gets ignored.
>
> What's the most painful repeated task in your users' workflow right now?

## Calibration Examples (what NOT to sound like)

- ❌ "Excited to share some thoughts on the AI revolution in SaaS! 🚀"
- ❌ "In today's ever-evolving landscape of artificial intelligence, SaaS companies must..."
- ❌ "Here are 10 ways to leverage AI to supercharge your SaaS..."
- ❌ "AI is a game-changer. Period."

## When Uncertain

If unsure about tone, lean **understated**. A flat, confident observation outperforms a hyped one nine times out of ten.

## 3. Likely Client Objections (and How to Handle Them)

### Objection 1: "$30K is expensive for something that doesn't include implementation"

**Response**: The $30K prevents you from spending $200K+ building the wrong AI features. The most expensive mistake in AI strategy isn't the assessment — it's the 6 months of engineering time wasted on features that don't solve real user problems. This is decision insurance.

### Objection 2: "We can figure this out internally — we know our users"

**Response**: Every team I've worked with believed they knew their pain points. Workflow mapping consistently reveals friction that's invisible from the inside. The AI opportunities that matter most are almost never on the existing roadmap. Internal teams have blind spots precisely because they're too close to the product.

### Objection 3: "We already have AI features / an AI roadmap"

**Response**: Great — that gives us a head start. But having AI features isn't the same as having the *right* AI features built on real workflow research. And it's definitely not the same as being agent-ready. Most teams I've seen have bolted on AI without understanding the underlying workflow friction. We audit what exists, identify gaps, and connect it into a coherent strategy.

### Objection 4: "You haven't done this exact engagement before — why should I trust you?"

**Response**: I've done every component of this engagement across multiple SaaS companies. At Jungle Scout, I led product for analytics tools serving Amazon sellers — deep workflow complexity, data-heavy decisions. At Scale Insights, I [specific relevant work]. At Data Dive, I [specific relevant work]. The framework is new. The skills and experience behind it are battle-tested across years of B2B SaaS product work.

### Objection 5: "8–12 weeks is too long — we need to move faster"

**Response**: Moving fast without clarity is how you waste 6 months. The 8–12 weeks is structured so your team has minimal disruption — most of the deep work happens on our side. And you walk away with artifacts you can act on immediately. The alternative is months of internal debate with no resolution.

### Objection 6: "Can't we just use ChatGPT / an AI tool to figure out our AI strategy?"

**Response**: AI tools are great at generating ideas. They're terrible at understanding your specific users' workflow friction, your data architecture, and your competitive moat. This isn't about generating a list of AI features. It's about understanding which problems are worth solving, in what order, with what data. That requires user research, workflow mapping, and deep product analysis — not prompts.

### Objection 7: "We're not sure AI agents are relevant to our market yet"

**Response**: That's exactly the window to prepare. By the time agents are visibly reshaping your market, the companies that prepared early will have already defined how agents interact with their category. The ones that waited will be trying to catch up. The assessment tells you exactly how exposed you are and what to do about it.

### Objection 8: "We're too small / too early for this"

**Response**: If you have real users, real data, and real workflows, you're not too early. The $500K–$5M ARR range is actually the sweet spot — you're big enough to have meaningful usage patterns but small enough to pivot your product strategy without massive organizational friction. Larger companies pay 10x for the same clarity and take 10x longer to act on it.

### Objection 9: "What if the blueprint tells us we don't need AI?"

**Response**: Then that's the most valuable $30K you'll ever spend. Knowing what *not* to build saves you from chasing trends that don't serve your users. But in practice, the assessment always uncovers high-impact opportunities — it's just that they're rarely the ones the team expected.

### Objection 10: "Can we start with a smaller engagement / pilot?"

**Response**: The value of the assessment comes from the complete picture — workflow audit, user research, data readiness, and the blueprint work together. A partial engagement gives you partial answers, which is exactly the problem you already have. That said, the discovery call is free and we'll be transparent about whether this is a fit before you commit.

### Objection 11: "We'd rather hire a full-time AI/ML person"

**Response**: A full-time hire solves implementation. This solves *what to implement*. You'd spend 3–6 months recruiting, onboarding, and then still need to figure out which AI features actually matter. The assessment gives that person (or your existing team) a clear mandate from day one. Many clients use the blueprint to write the job description for their AI hire.

### Objection 12: "Our investors / board want to see us shipping AI features, not doing assessments"

**Response**: Boards want to see AI *progress*, and shipping the wrong features is worse than shipping nothing. The assessment gives you a board-ready strategic narrative: here's the opportunity, here's the plan, here's the priority order, here's why. That's more convincing than "we added a chatbot."

## 4. Cold Outbound Strategy

### Lead Sources

#### A. AppSumo & Product Hunt — Legacy Launches
- **AppSumo**: Search for B2B SaaS tools launched 2–4 years ago. These are exactly the "legacy" tools that shipped for human users and haven't adapted. Many are in the $500K–$5M ARR range.
  - Look at tools in categories: project management, CRM, analytics, workflow automation, developer tools
  - Find the founder on LinkedIn, check their company size, and reach out
- **Product Hunt**: Search archived launches (2020–2023) for B2B SaaS products. Filter for products that got traction but aren't AI-native.
  - Use the Product Hunt API or manual search
  - Cross-reference with LinkedIn to find the founder/CEO

#### B. SaaS Directories & Databases
- **G2/Capterra**: Browse B2B SaaS categories, find products with reviews that mention workflow friction, missing features, or requests for automation
- **Crunchbase**: Filter for B2B SaaS companies, Series A or bootstrapped, $500K–$5M revenue range
- **SaaSHub**: Directory of SaaS alternatives — great for finding mid-market tools
- **PitchBook** (if you have access): Filter by SaaS, revenue range, employee count (15–80 is your sweet spot)

#### C. LinkedIn Sales Navigator
- Search for: Founder/CEO/CPO/VP Product at B2B SaaS companies, 10–80 employees
- Filter by industry, company size, and keywords
- Build a prospect list and use connection requests + personalized messages

#### D. Indie Hacker / Bootstrapper Communities
- **IndieHackers.com**: Many founders in the $500K–$5M range are active here. Engage in discussions, then DM when relevant.
- **MicroConf community**: The exact demographic — bootstrapped/lightly funded SaaS founders hitting growth ceilings
- **SaaStr community**: More VC-backed but still relevant

#### E. Job Boards as Intent Signals
- Companies posting for "AI Product Manager," "ML Engineer," or "AI Strategy" roles are literally signaling that they need what you offer but haven't figured out the strategy yet
- Monitor: LinkedIn Jobs, Wellfound (AngelList), Otta, Y Combinator's Work at a Startup
- Reach out to the founder: "I saw you're hiring for [AI role]. Before you bring someone in, have you mapped out which AI opportunities are worth pursuing?"

#### F. SaaS Conference Attendee Lists
- SaaStr, MicroConf, SaaS North, etc. — attendee lists or speaker lists are gold
- These people are actively investing in growth and strategy

## 5. Additional Lead Generation Channels

### A. Content Marketing Beyond LinkedIn
- **Write 2–3 long-form articles** on your website blog or Medium/Substack:
  - "Why B2B SaaS Companies Need an AI Agent Strategy (Not Just AI Features)"
  - "The Workflow-First Approach to AI Product Strategy"
  - "What I Learned About AI Readiness from [X] Years in SaaS Product"
- These serve as evergreen assets you can link from LinkedIn, emails, and your profile

### B. Podcast Guesting
- Pitch yourself to B2B SaaS podcasts (SaaS Club, Startups For the Rest of Us, The SaaS Podcast, Build Your SaaS, etc.)
- Your angle: "Why most SaaS AI features fail and what to do instead"
- This is a massive trust builder for a $30K service

### C. Strategic Partnerships
- **Fractional CTOs / Technical Advisors**: They work with exactly your ICP and can refer you. You handle strategy, they handle implementation. No competition.
- **SaaS-focused VCs and angels**: They want their portfolio companies to have AI strategy. Offer to do a workshop or write a brief for their portfolio.
- **Design agencies and dev shops**: They build features but don't do strategy. Refer each other.

### D. Free Workshop / Webinar
- Host a monthly 45-min session: "Is Your SaaS Ready for AI Agents? A Self-Assessment Framework"
- Give away 70% of the thinking, keep the deep work as the paid engagement
- Capture emails, follow up with attendees who match ICP

### E. Community Engagement
- Be genuinely helpful in SaaS founder communities (not pitch-y)
- Answer AI strategy questions, share frameworks, build reputation
- When someone asks "should we add AI to our product?" — that's your moment

### F. Case Study / Proof of Concept
- Your biggest gap right now is social proof for *this specific engagement*
- Consider offering your first 1–2 clients at a reduced rate ($15–20K) in exchange for a detailed case study and testimonial
- Alternatively, create a "sample deliverable" based on a public SaaS product (anonymized or with permission) to show what the output looks like
